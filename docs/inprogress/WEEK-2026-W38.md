**★★ THE AUDIO STALL WAS CAPTURED — three times, first ever** (2026-09-18), and **two bugs were fixed

✅ **Fixed, and it fired on 100% of boots:** `audio_pwmInit()` wrote `PWM_CTL` three times back to
back — the BCM doc's own BERR trigger — so the driver latched a **bus error every boot** with nothing
decoding bit 8 to notice. Paced now; **BERR clear across 78 boots + a 6/6 gate** (devices `11f182a`,

✅ **Also fixed:** picocom kills its process group — that had been killing every boot-only bench at

📊 **Then measured, not poked: 60 boots on the frozen gated build — 0 stalls** (p ≈ 0.015 vs the
pre-pacing 3-in-44). ⚠ A **bound, not a cure**: pacing changed init timing, and this fault's rate
moves with timing — it already went 3-in-44 → 0-in-127 across a *functionally neutral* relink. The
mechanism stays open.
ⓘ **Net for the demo: nothing on screen changes.** The stall was always contained and the games gate
6/6 either way. Full record: [`2026-09-18-audio-dma-stall-captures.md`](../misc/2026-09-18-audio-dma-stall-captures.md).
✅ **CONTAINED — the thing a live demo would notice is gone** (devices `5cd9cc3`, `a7e7e54`-class
re-arm + clock): the driver called a **parked** channel "started", so it now grades by **progress**
(healthy **1 784-2 352** ring words per 20 ms, threshold 64), re-arms the PWM up to 3× printing the
full engine state, and otherwise degrades `/dev/audio0` to a **paced null sink** — app runs silent,
nothing blocks. **Negative control 10/10 boots: arm 1, 0 re-arms, 0 null sinks, 6/6 stages.**
↩ **And it corrected the capture record's own headline:** `DMA_CS=0x21` is **not** a stall signature —
**9 of 10 healthy boots read it while streaming**. The channel is DREQ-paced, so it is *always* held
between DREQs (Linux calls bit 5 `ISHELD`). Only "0 samples" and the full ring ever meant anything.
⊕ **Root-cause candidate landed on upstream evidence, not claimed as a cure:** our PWM **clock** start
changed the source mux and the enable on **one bus cycle** and wrote DIV before CTL, where
`clk-bcm2835.c` deliberately splits them ("pause clock generation while updating the control and div
regs") and always sets `CM_GATE`, which we never did. A race by construction — which is what a ~7%
failure with every register reading back correct looks like.

**Same method, next defect — no capture yet:** 10 long STK races on the frozen build (6-lap profiles,
**31 806 frames**, the workload 3 of the 4 archived heap-guard fires came from) — **0 fires, 0
faults**. A bound with its exposure stated, not a clearance. What is banked: the shipping build is now
sampled with `why=`/`lheap?`/`freed?` in place, so the next fire says in one line whether a block
outlived its heap, a stale pointer's address got reused, or `->heap` was smashed.
